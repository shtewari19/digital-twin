## 4. Detailed Interpretation

### Interpretation of Results

The winning message, "Fixed 18-month treatment duration with subcutaneous dosing — giving your patients certainty and your practice predictability," resonated strongly across the surveyed cohorts, achieving the highest brand trust (BT) strength of 0.714. This message effectively addresses two critical pain points for oncologists: the desire for treatment consistency and the need for predictable practice management. The emphasis on a fixed treatment duration provides a sense of stability for both patients and healthcare providers, allowing oncologists to plan their treatment regimens without the uncertainty often associated with variable therapy durations. Additionally, the subcutaneous dosing format enhances convenience and aligns with the trend toward outpatient care, making it a compelling proposition for oncologists who prioritize patient comfort and operational efficiency.

Conversely, the lowest-ranked message, "Proven remission in triple-class exposed patients who have failed BCMA-targeted therapy — with no Grade 3+ CRS observed in the Phase 3 trial," underperformed with a BT strength of only 0.143. While the clinical efficacy and safety data presented are undoubtedly important, the message may have lacked the emotional appeal and practical relevance that oncologists seek. The focus on complex clinical outcomes may not resonate as strongly with practitioners who are more concerned with the day-to-day implications of treatment options. Furthermore, the terminology used (e.g., "triple-class exposed" and "BCMA-targeted therapy") may have alienated some oncologists who are less familiar with these specific treatment pathways, thereby limiting the message's overall accessibility and impact.

Differentiation across persona families reveals nuanced preferences that can inform targeted messaging strategies. For instance, the "Community Oncologist — Rural Network" and "Community Oncologist — Suburban Early Adopter" both ranked the winning message as their top preference, indicating a universal appeal for the promise of certainty and predictability in treatment. However, the "Community Oncologist — Independent Practice" cohort showed a slightly higher average score for the message emphasizing CAR-T-like efficacy, suggesting that this group may place greater value on clinical outcomes and efficacy data. This differentiation highlights the importance of tailoring communication strategies to address the specific needs and preferences of each persona family, ensuring that messaging resonates effectively across diverse oncologist profiles.
